E-Bike Essentials

  • EU law: Max 250W motor, 25 km/h assist cutoff. Above = moped (registration + insurance).
  • Range: Plan 60–100 km/charge. Carry a spare or plan charging every 80 km at cafes/hostels.
  • Ferries: Bikes treated as regular bikes. Some charge extra for the battery.
  • Train (Klaipėda→Warsaw via Vilnius overnight): Day 1: LTG Link to Vilnius (~4.5 hrs) — book bike space in advance. Overnight in Vilnius Old Town. Day 2: LTG Link to Warsaw (~7.5 hrs) — get off here and begin the Poland cycling leg directly. No onward connection to Gdańsk needed. Total ~€65 inc. bike fees.
  • GPX files: Download from EuroVelo, Komoot, and Bikemap for each named route. Essential in Latvia/Lithuania/Poland where signage is patchy.

⛺ Free Camping Guide by Country

✓ Legal — Right to Roam
SwedenAllemansrätten
Camp anywhere for 1–2 nights except private gardens, cultivated fields, and within sight of dwellings. No permission needed. Fires only where safe and permitted (check local fire bans in summer). The most cyclist-friendly camping law in Europe.
Finland / ÅlandJokamiehenoikeus
Everyman’s Right: camp anywhere on uncultivated land, keep 150m from buildings. No fires without landowner permission, but many designated fire pits in forests. RMK-style shelters are less common than Estonia but laavu (lean-to) shelters exist in many national parks.
Estonia1-night rule
Legal to camp 1 night in forests and public land. RMK (State Forest) shelters are superb — free wooden shelters with fire pits, dry toilet, and firewood at 60+ locations across the country. Map them at loodusegakoos.ee. The best free camping infrastructure on the route.
⚠ Tolerated — Grey Area (Use Common Sense)
LatviaTolerated
No legal right to camp, but widely practiced in forests. Technically need landowner permission. Nobody checks. Stay off private farmland and you’ll have zero issues.
Best spots: dense pine forests behind coastal dunes between Saulkrasti and Ventspils. Cape Kolka area is especially wild and empty.
LithuaniaTolerated
Similar to Latvia. Forest camping tolerated. Some forests have free laužavietė (sheltered camping spots with fire pits). Palanga pine forests near the coast are excellent.
MontenegroTolerated
No specific anti-camping law. Tolerated in mountains and rural areas. Coastal areas are busier — camp away from beaches and towns.
Best spots: above the Bay of Kotor on Lovćen approaches. Lake Skadar north shore has empty stretches. Mountain passes are ideal.
AlbaniaTolerated / Welcomed
No real enforcement anywhere. Locals often invite you to camp in their yard or field. Albania is arguably the most camping-friendly country in Europe by culture. Extremely safe.
Camp literally anywhere rural. In towns, ask at a café and someone will find you a spot. Albanian hospitality (besa) is legendary.
N. Macedonia / KosovoTolerated
No enforcement. Camp in rural/mountain areas without issue. Lake Ohrid lakeshore has some designated spots. Kosovo is very welcoming to travelers.
Serbia / BosniaTolerated
Technically need permission, never enforced in rural areas. Mountain camping is completely accepted. Zlatibor and Tara regions (Serbia) are excellent. Bosnia’s river valleys and mountain passes are ideal.
Neretva canyon between Sarajevo and Mostar: find a flat spot above the river. Stunning wild camps.
✗ Illegal — Campsites or Stealth Required
PolandIllegal
Wild camping prohibited outside designated areas. However, Poland has hundreds of cheap campsites (€5–12/night) and some forests have designated bivouac spots (biwak). The Vistula riverbanks have many hidden spots cyclists use.
Stealth: dense forest patches along the Vistula between towns. Set up late, leave early. Vistula river islands are excellent — nobody goes there. App: park4night for spots other cyclists have used.
Czech RepublicIllegal
Camping outside campsites is banned. Some forests have designated bivouac areas (tábořiště) marked on mapy.cz. Budget auto-camps €6–15. Moravia has many small sites near wine cellars.
Stealth: the Moravian forests between Olomouc and Brno. Use mapy.cz (Czech app) to find forest tracks. Camp well off trails.
AustriaStrictly Illegal
FINED if caught. Austria takes this seriously, especially in Tyrol and national parks. Excellent campsite network (€12–22/night). Some Alm (alpine pasture) farmers allow camping if you ask.
Stealth (risky): deep Styrian forests between Vienna and Graz. NEVER near trails, roads, or villages. Late arrival, early departure essential. Your best bet: negotiate directly with farmers — many will say yes for €5–10.
SloveniaIllegal
Banned except at campsites. Eco-farms (turistične kmetije) sometimes allow camping — check Slovenia tourism site. Budget campsites €10–18.
Stealth: the Karst region between Ljubljana and Postojna has deep limestone forest. Remote enough that enforcement is nonexistent. Also: some mountain huts (koča) allow tent camping nearby for a small fee.
CroatiaIllegal
Strictly illegal on the coast (frequent police patrols in summer). Massive auto-camp network — Croatia has 500+ registered campsites, many directly on the coast from €10–20/night. Inland Istria and islands are more relaxed.
Stealth: Istrian interior olive groves (ask farmers). Between Split and Dubrovnik: find spots above the road where mountain meets coast — terraced olive/vine land is often abandoned and usable. Pelješac peninsula interior is very quiet. The Neretva delta has secluded spots.

🐘 Wildlife Risks
TORMENTED BY TICKS — #1 Threat (Entire Route)
Ticks carrying Lyme disease and TBE (tick-borne encephalitis) are present across your entire route. The highest-risk zones are the Baltics (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania are among Europe’s worst), southern Sweden, Finland, Poland, and Czech Republic.

Prevention: Treat clothing with permethrin before departure. Wear long pants tucked into socks when walking through grass. Full body tick check every evening — they prefer warm folds (groin, armpits, behind ears, waistband).

TBE Vaccine: Get vaccinated before departure. 2-dose course (Encepur or FSME-Immun), available in Australia. Lyme has no vaccine — rely on tick checks and removal within 24hrs.

Removal: Carry a tick removal tool (O’Tom tick twister or fine-point tweezers). Grip at the head, twist gently, pull straight out. Do not squeeze the body. Clean with antiseptic. Monitor for expanding red ring (Lyme indicator) over next 2–4 weeks.
SAVAGED BY SNAKES — Two Species to Know
European Adder (Hugorm/Kyy) — Sweden → Baltics → Poland
The only venomous snake in Scandinavia and the Baltics. Zigzag pattern on back, 50–70cm. Very shy, bites are rare and almost never fatal. Found in meadows, forest edges, and rocky ground. Risk: low. Stomp your feet when walking through undergrowth and they’ll flee.

Nose-Horned Viper (Poskok) — Slovenia → Croatia → all Balkans
Europe’s most dangerous snake. Thick body, triangular head, distinctive horn on snout, up to 90cm. Found in rocky karst terrain, dry stone walls, and sun-warmed rubble — exactly the type of terrain on the Croatian and Bosnian coast. This is a genuine concern when choosing camp spots in the Balkans.

Camp smart: Clear ground before pitching. Never reach into rock crevices or under stones. Shake out shoes and gear in the morning. In Croatia/Bosnia/Montenegro, prefer grassy clearings over rocky ground. The viper is nocturnal in summer — use a headtorch walking at night.
BATTERED BY BEARS
Present in Finland (~2,000), Estonia (small population), Slovenia (~1,000 — highest density in western Europe), Croatia (Gorski Kotar/Velebit), Bosnia, Montenegro, Albania, N. Macedonia, and Kosovo.

European brown bears are very shy and avoid humans. Attacks on campers are exceptionally rare (<1/year across all of Europe). They are NOT comparable to North American grizzlies.

Camp practice: Store food in panniers away from your tent (hang from a branch or seal in dry bags). Cook and eat 50m+ from your sleeping spot. Never keep food or strong-smelling items (toothpaste, sunscreen) in the tent. In Slovenia’s Notranjska forests, bears are common but almost never seen — they hear you long before you see them. Make noise on forest trails.
BULLDOZED BY BOARS
Common in forests across the entire route from Sweden to Bosnia. The most likely large animal you’ll actually encounter. Usually harmless and will run, but a sow with piglets can charge if she feels cornered.

Camp practice: Keep food sealed and away from tent. If you hear grunting/snuffling at night, bang pots or shout — they’ll leave. Don’t camp on obvious boar trails (look for churned-up earth and tracks). Most encounters happen at dusk.
MASSACRED BY MOZZIES
Scandinavia & Finland (Jun–Aug): Can be brutal, especially near lakes and in the Finnish interior. Carry DEET 30%+ or Icaridin repellent. A head net weighs nothing and can save your sanity. Your tent’s mesh is your best friend.

Baltics: Coastal marshes in Latvia and Lithuania have heavy mosquitoes in summer. Camp on higher ground or windward coastal spots where breeze keeps them away.

Poland (Vistula): River valleys breed mosquitoes. Camp away from standing water.

Balkans: Less intense than Scandinavia but present near rivers and lakes. Horse flies (obadi) are a nuisance on hot days in Bosnia/Montenegro.
MOWED DOWN BY MOOSE (Sweden & Finland)
Sweden has ~350,000 moose, Finland ~100,000. They are a road hazard, not a camping threat. Most dangerous at dusk and dawn when they cross roads. A moose collision at cycling speed can cause serious injury — they’re 400–700kg.

Cycling: Be alert at dawn/dusk on forest roads. Use lights. If one crosses in front of you, stop — where there’s one, there’s often a second.
Camping: If a moose wanders into camp, stay calm, give it space, and it will move on. Never approach calves.
DEVOURED BY DOGS (Balkans)
The most frequent actual confrontation you’ll face. Stray dogs are common in Albania, Kosovo, N. Macedonia, Serbia, and Bosnia. Rural guard dogs (often Sharplaninac or Tornjak breeds) protect livestock and can be territorial.

While cycling: Dogs will chase bikes. Stop pedaling, dismount, and walk slowly. Put the bike between you and the dog. Avoid eye contact. Speak calmly. They almost always lose interest once you stop being a moving target.
While camping: Keep food sealed. If dogs visit camp at night, a firm loud voice usually works. Carry a stick for confidence. Strays in Albanian towns are generally docile and socialized — rural livestock guardians are the ones to watch for.
Gear: Some bikepackers carry a dog deterrent spray (citronella-based, legal in EU). A small ultrasonic dog deterrent device (Dazer II) weighs 70g and works well.
SKEWERED BY SCORPIONS (Adriatic Coast)
Small Mediterranean scorpions (Euscorpius spp.) are found along the Croatian, Montenegrin, and Albanian coasts. They hide under rocks and in dry stone walls. Sting is painful but not medically dangerous — similar to a bee sting.

Camp practice: Shake out shoes, clothing, and panniers every morning. Don’t place bare hands under rocks. Keep tent zipped at all times.
⚠ BLOWN UP IN BOSNIA — LANDMINE AREAS
Not wildlife, but a critical camping safety note. Bosnia has residual landmines from the 1992–95 war, primarily along former front lines. Affected areas are marked with red signs or red-painted rocks. Never camp off established trails or roads in Bosnia. Stick to paved roads, well-used paths, and cleared areas. The Neretva valley main road corridor is safe. Mountain shortcuts through unmarked forest are not. This is a real and ongoing risk — casualties still occur.
